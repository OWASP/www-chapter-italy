# OWASP Precogly: Open-Source Threat Modeling for the AI-Coding Era

## Vikramaditya Narayan

As software development becomes increasingly AI-assisted, threat models must remain structured, reproducible, auditable, and understandable by humans.

This session introduces [OWASP Precogly](https://github.com/OWASP/Precogly), an open-source platform designed to support repeatable and AI-agent-ready threat modeling.

The presentation will explore how Precogly combines visual data-flow modelling with structured libraries that connect system components, threats, and countermeasures to established sources such as STRIDE, CWE, CAPEC, and MITRE ATT&CK.

Participants will see how Precogly can support:

* visual creation of data-flow diagrams;
* systematic identification and management of threats;
* reusable and community-curated threat-model libraries;
* traceability between threats, countermeasures, and compliance requirements;
* TM-BOM import and export;
* integration with engineering workflows and AI agents;
* human review of AI-assisted threat-model outputs.

The session will show how an open-source approach can help organizations move from occasional threat-modeling exercises to a continuous and maintainable engineering practice.

### Speaker bio

Vikramaditya Narayan is the creator and Project Leader of OWASP Precogly, an open-source platform for repeatable and AI-agent-ready threat modeling.

He holds a Master’s degree from Carnegie Mellon University and is a Certified Threat Modeling Professional and Azure-certified AI Engineer. His work focuses on the intersection of threat modeling, AI-assisted software development, AI agents, and security engineering.

# From Cyber Resilience Act Requirements to Engineering Practice: The Role of OWASP

## Elena Musetti

The EU Cyber Resilience Act introduces a new regulatory framework for products with digital elements, placing cybersecurity responsibilities directly on manufacturers throughout the product lifecycle.

Compliance cannot be treated as a final certification exercise. Manufacturers need to translate legal requirements into repeatable software-engineering, vulnerability-management, documentation, and assurance activities.

This presentation will provide an introduction to the Cyber Resilience Act and examine how OWASP projects can help organizations operationalize some of its core requirements.

The session will address:

* the scope of the Cyber Resilience Act and the products and organizations involved;
* cybersecurity-by-design and cybersecurity-by-default obligations;
* secure development and verification throughout the product lifecycle;
* vulnerability handling, coordinated vulnerability disclosure, remediation, and security updates;
* software-component transparency and the role of SBOM and VEX;
* technical documentation and the evidence needed to demonstrate conformity;
* the relationship between regulatory requirements, standards, and open-source security practices.

Particular attention will be given to the practical contribution of OWASP projects, including:

* **OWASP SAMM**, for assessing and improving secure software-development practices;
* **OWASP ASVS**, for defining and verifying application-security requirements;
* **OWASP CycloneDX**, for software-component transparency, SBOM, and VEX;
* **OWASP Software Component Verification Standard**, for managing risks associated with third-party components;
* **OWASP Testing Guides and Cheat Sheet Series**, for supporting technical verification and secure implementation.

The objective is not to present OWASP resources as a substitute for legal analysis or conformity assessment, but to show how they can provide manufacturers and development teams with concrete, openly available foundations for implementing and documenting cybersecurity practices.

The talk will also open a broader discussion on how collaboration between OWASP, standards-development organizations, European institutions, industry, and research communities could make the CRA transition more effective and technically grounded.

### Speaker bio

Elena Musetti works on the intersection of cybersecurity, European regulation, and software-security practices, with a particular focus on the Cyber Resilience Act.

Her activities explore how regulatory requirements can be translated into concrete and sustainable processes for product manufacturers and software-development organizations, and how open standards and OWASP projects can support this transition.
